What is the best password manager in 2026?

The best manager is the one that is independent, audited, supports passkeys and that you will actually keep using. Here is how the leading options compare.

Quick answer: For most people, a well-audited open-source manager such as Bitwarden or Vaultwarden is the safest default, with 1Password and Proton Pass as strong paid alternatives. Look for independent audits, passkey support, and zero-knowledge encryption before choosing.

What should I look for in a password manager?

  • Zero-knowledge encryption - the provider cannot read your vault.
  • Independent security audits - published, recent, and from a known firm.
  • Passkey support - the most phishing-resistant second factor.
  • Secure sync - end-to-end encrypted across devices.
  • Emergency access and sharing - for families and teams.

Is a built-in browser password manager good enough?

Often, yes. Modern browser managers encrypt vaults with a device key and sync through your OS account. The trade-off is that they are tied to one ecosystem and offer fewer sharing features. If you want cross-platform use and sharing, a standalone manager is stronger.

What if I forget my master password?

With zero-knowledge encryption, the provider cannot reset it. Keep a securely stored physical recovery kit or emergency-access contact. Write the master password on paper stored somewhere safe if that is your preference, and never reuse it anywhere else.

How do I move passwords into a manager safely?

Import the CSV export from your current manager, then verify every entry, delete the exported CSV immediately, and reset any accounts you cannot confidently verify. Browser exports are plain text, so treat the file as a live secret until it is deleted.
