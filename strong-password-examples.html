What do strong password examples look like?

Strong passwords come in two flavours: long random strings generated by a tool, and multi-word passphrases you can remember. Here are real examples with their bit counts.

Quick answer: A passphrase such as "velvet-orchid-9-tundra" and a random string such as "k7#Rp2vLm9$QxTd" both clear 70+ bits. A human-invented complex password like "Tr0ub4dor&3" often lands under 50.

Why is a passphrase often better than random characters?

It carries comparable entropy at a fraction of the memorability cost, and it is far less likely to end up in a wordlist because the combination of unrelated words is what matters. Four or five random words is the sweet spot.

How do I make a passphrase that is actually hard to guess?

Choose words with no relationship to each other and no connection to your life. Avoid your hometown, your pet, your employer, and anything visible in your social posts. A diceware list of five words reliably clears 60 bits.

Should I use symbols in a passphrase?

Only if the service demands them. A separator or a digit between words adds a little entropy and often satisfies the rule, whereas a mangled word like "P@ssw0rd" reduces memorability while adding almost none.
